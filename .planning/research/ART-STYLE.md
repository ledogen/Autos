# Art Style: what AUTOS looks like

**Read this before modelling anything.** It is the visual brief. `ASSETS.md` (sibling file) covers
the mechanical conventions — format, paths, export settings, pivots. This file covers what the thing
should *look* like, so you don't have to be told.

Verified against the three shipped models and the procedural prop system as of 2026-08-05.

---

## The one-line brief

**Flat-coloured low-poly, faceted, no textures. Detail is geometry, never a map. Desaturated
natural world; saturated man-made objects. Read at 20 m through fog, not at 2 m in a turntable.**

If you only remember one thing: **anything you would normally paint into a texture, you either
model or you leave out.**

## Rules

### 1. Flat colour per material. No textures.

Every shipped model except `news-roll` and `produce-stall` has **zero images**. A material is a `baseColorFactor` and
nothing else — no albedo map, no normal map, no roughness map, no AO map, no emissive map.

Texture only when it carries **information that geometry cannot**: printed text, a label, a logo, a
photograph. "It would look richer" is not information. If you are reaching for a texture to add wear,
grain, panel lines or dirt, you are reaching for the wrong tool — model it or drop it.

**Lettering is the standing exception, and it pays for itself.** `produce-stall` (owner ruling
2026-08-18, citing `news-roll` as precedent) drew its sign's letters as geometry first: 784 tris,
**29% of the whole model**, and still no grain and no two-tone shadowed letterform. One 512×384
image replaced all of it. If an asset needs readable words on it, reach for the texture — but for
the *words*, not for the surface around them.

`gas-pump` (2026-08-22) is the cleanest statement of the same rule, because its ticket budgeted one
texture for *four* things — dial face, faded livery, rust streaks, price digits — and only the
printed ones survived contact with this section. Livery and rust are wear, the named anti-pattern,
and they were dropped. The dial and its digits stayed, on the owner's call, because a pump register
is *printed information* in exactly the sense this rule buys: a cream face, three register windows,
their captions, and a red GASOLINE band, none of which geometry can say at 460 tris.

**When a model needs two lettered surfaces, atlas them.** `gas-pump` puts GAS on the top strip of a
512×512 and the gauge face on the lower block, with a 32 px gutter between so minification cannot
bleed one into the other. One image, one material, one draw call, instead of two of each. The trap
to know: lay the artwork out in **atlas units** — the unit square that maps to the image — never in
per-region normalised coordinates, because the regions have different aspects and region-normalised
drawing stretches one artwork and not the other. Derive each plate's aspect from its region's pixel
size in the same file, so the mesh and the artwork can never drift apart.

**Turn the render dither off when you bake flat artwork.** Blender ships
`scene.render.dither_intensity = 1.0`, which stipples every flat region with ±1 LSB noise so that
8-bit *gradients* do not band. Baked sign artwork has no gradients — it is four flat colours — and
that noise defeats PNG's row filters completely. On the ASSET-31 signs it cost **230 kB per face**,
which was most of each `.glb`; setting it to `0.0` took the set of seven from 1.6 MB to 196 kB. Set
it in the bake scene, next to `view_transform = 'Standard'`. (`gas-pump.glb` predates this and
still carries the penalty.)

**Turn the render stamps off too.** Blender writes render metadata — the date, the render time — into
the baked PNG's text chunks, so every re-run of a generator produces a different `.glb` even when not
one pixel moved, and "re-export once to prove it is byte-identical" (ASSETS.md, promotion) can never
pass. `general-store.py` (ASSET-74) found it: the only bytes that differed between two exports were
`Date` and `RenderTime`. Clear every `scene.render.use_stamp_*` flag in the bake scene.

**Generate the artwork, don't paint it.** `produce-stall.py` bakes its own sign: flat emitters laid
out in board metres, rendered through an orthographic camera, packed into the `.blend`. Re-running
the generator reproduces the texture along with the mesh, so the asset stays a one-command rebuild
and the layout numbers stay in the same file as the geometry. Hand-painted PNGs that live only in
`assets/` are the thing to avoid — they cannot be regenerated when a board's proportions change.

**Per-piece tint is vertex colour, not a second material** (owner, 2026-10-06, `pallet.glb`). When
one object is many like pieces — boards, slats, planks — and one flat colour reads as plastic, give
each piece a tint in a `COLOR_0` vertex-colour attribute. glTF multiplies it into `baseColorFactor`,
`GLTFLoader` turns `vertexColors` on by itself, and it stays **one material, one draw call** — where
three wood materials would cost three per placed copy. Rules: author the material at the *lightest*
piece and keep every tint ≤ 1 (a multiply only darkens); write it as a linear `FLOAT_COLOR` on the
corner domain so each piece stays flat; export with `export_vertex_color="ACTIVE"` (the default
`MATERIAL` mode silently drops it unless a Color Attribute node is wired in). It is for variation
*between pieces*, never a gradient, wear or shading painted across a surface — that is still a texture
by another name. Runtime palette recolour still works: it swaps the factor, the tints ride on top.

> **Trap (Blender 5.2, found 2026-10-09): the glTF exporter writes `COLOR_0` only for a mesh's
> FIRST material slot.** Every later primitive of a multi-material mesh exports with all-white
> vertex colours, in every `export_vertex_color` mode — so a tinted second material (the crate's
> produce, a case's cans, a board's end grain) silently loses its tints and renders flat. `pallet.py`
> never hit it because the pallet is one material. A tinted model with several materials must be
> split into **one object per material under an empty root** before export (`split_by_material()`
> in the `wip/freight/freight.py` generator is the reference); the draw-call count is unchanged.

This is not just taste. It costs no texture memory, needs no bake step, needs no UVs at all, and it
makes runtime recolour free (see `ASSETS.md`, "Untextured assets").

### 2. Detail is geometry.

The trailer's lap siding is 13 courses of shallow V-grooves. Its curtains are folded zigzags. Its
skirting has real battens and a real recessed vent. All of it is polygons, and it costs about what a
texture would have cost in memory while surviving any light direction.

The trick that makes this work: **flat shading turns a fold into a colour change for free.** A groove
or a pleat reads as light/dark banding purely from facet normals — one material, no texture, 2 tris a
fold. Use it constantly.

### 3. Faceted. Never smooth-shade.

Everything is flat-shaded. Props are built non-indexed so `computeVertexNormals()` yields per-face
normals; the trailer sets `f.smooth = False` on every face. Do not add smoothing groups, do not
average vertex normals, do not raise subdivision to hide faceting. **The facets are the look.**

### 4. Budgets by role

Real budgets from the shipped asset tickets:

| Role | Tris | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Small scatter / repeated dressing | **250–500** | mailbox, crate, pallet, gnome, beach ball |
| Hand props, camp gear, cargo | **350–700** | newspaper roll (358), cooking kit, propane tank |
| Mid structures, furniture clusters | **700–1800** | awning, produce stall, fire pit |
| POI buildings, vehicles | **1200–4000** | trailer home (1192), hilux (1908), sawmill, gas station |
| **The hero vehicle** (one asset) | **4000** | `ranger.glb` (2910) — budgeted by hours looked at, not by repeats |

Budget by **how often it repeats**, not by how important it feels. One ticket puts it well: *"it will
be the most-repeated object in the game; treat the budget as hard."*

Report the **evaluated** count through the depsgraph with modifiers applied, not the base mesh.

### 5. Colour: desaturated world, saturated things people made

The nature palette is grey-green-brown and close to neutral. Man-made objects carry the saturation
and are what your eye lands on. That contrast is the reason a trailer reads as a destination from
150 m away.

    NATURAL (from data/flora.js)
      aspen bark   #eae7df   fleck #242424      pine bark   #5a3d22
      aspen canopy #86b84a                      pine canopy #3b6840
      rock/boulder #b7b6b4   variant #8a8782    deadwood    #6f5c46
      bush         #4f8a3a

    MAN-MADE (from trailer-home-a, linear values)
      body    0.28 0.72 0.76      accent  0.10 0.34 0.40
      roof    0.34 0.34 0.35      skirt   0.16 0.15 0.14
      trim    0.76 0.73 0.63      door    0.45 0.33 0.24
      curtain 0.82 0.65 0.52      metal   0.14 0.15 0.16

Give an object a **value structure**, not just hues: something dark at the base to sit it down,
something light to catch the eye, one warm accent. The trailer is dark skirt → off-white belt →
saturated body → mid-grey roof, bottom to top.

Keep metalness at **0** and vary **roughness** instead (0.45 metalwork → 0.95 fabric). No shipped
asset uses metalness above 0.2.

> **Trap: these are LINEAR values.** glTF `baseColorFactor` is linear, and it renders roughly 1.5×
> lighter than the number suggests — 0.26 linear is about 0.55 sRGB, a mid grey. A "dark grey" that
> looks right as a tuple will render mid-grey. Pick dark values far lower than seems right, and
> **judge them rendered, never from the number.**

### 6. Draw calls = materials. Count them.

Three.js issues one draw call per material. Fine for a POI placed a handful of times; **not** fine
for anything scatter-density, which must share one material and go through the instancing path.

Merge materials that are the same colour and same role. Only split when the two surfaces need to be
tuned or recoloured independently — and when you do split, say so in the ticket and say why.

### 7. Glass and interiors are allowed — flat clear materials, minimal interiors.

Glass, film and other see-through surfaces are fine (owner, 2026-10-06). The precedents in the game:

| Material | Asset | Opacity | Sides |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CarGlass` | `broken-car.glb` | 0.72 | double |
| `RVGlass` | `winnebago.glb` | 0.45 | double |
| `RangerGlass` | `ranger.glb` | 0.45 | double |
| `BundleWrap` | `firewood-bundle-*.glb`, `splitting-yard.glb` | 0.14 | single |

The rules that still hold:

- **`alphaMode: BLEND` on a flat colour + opacity.** No alpha textures and no cutout (`MASK` /
  `alphaTest`) foliage: textures are out anyway, and cutout foliage is a texture.
- **One clear material per asset.** Three.js draws transparent objects after the opaque ones, sorted
  back to front each frame, so a clear material never merges into the opaque draws. One is cheap;
  several on one asset add up.
- **Pick the opacity by what sits behind it.** 0.45 over a modelled interior or curtains (at 0.72
  the RV's curtains blacked out), 0.72 over a dark tub, 0.14 for a thin film.
- **Double-sided only when you look through it from both sides** (a window you also look *out* of).
  A closed wrap stays single-sided, or its far wall draws on top of its near wall.
- **Opaque still wins when it reads as well.** The trailer has no glass on purpose: pleated curtains
  behind an opening sell a window with no sort order to get wrong.
- **Mind the count on repeated things.** Every placed copy is a separately sorted object. A POI or a
  bed of 15 bundles is fine; check the frame cost before putting a clear material on anything
  scatter-density.

**Interiors are allowed too — only as much as the glass shows, and hide the rest on purpose**
(owner, 2026-10-06). Glass over an empty shell reads worse than no glass, so a window that looks into
a space gets just enough inside to read as occupied. Everything the player can't usefully see is
closed off deliberately rather than modelled:

- **Section it off.** The RV (`winnebago.glb`) models only the cab. The camper behind it is sealed:
  its windows sit proud of a closed hull with no hole behind them.
- **Draw the blinds.** The trailer (`trailer-home-a.glb`) has no glass at all. Closed pleated
  curtains sit behind each opening, so there is nothing inside to model.
- **Model to the sightline, not to reality.** Anything meant to be seen through a window must break
  the beltline, and interior surfaces go near-black so only the value gap reads (see the worked
  examples below). Below the sill and behind a partition, stop.

How far to go is set by how close and how long the player looks. The broken car gets a tub and two
benches. The hero truck, which the player sits inside, gets a full cockpit.

### 8. Silhouette first — you are seen through fog

The camera is a car windscreen, not a turntable. Verified viewing conditions:

- Terrain draws to about **160 m** (chunk ring 2 × 64 m tiles).
- `FogExp2` at density 0.006 (Normal preset): **~60% fogged at 160 m**, ~30% at 100 m, 96% by 300 m.
- `ACESFilmicToneMapping` applies to the whole scene, so values compress at the top end.

So: **silhouette and value structure survive; surface detail does not.** Spend polys on the outline
and on big light/dark divisions. A 0.03 m panel line is invisible past ~15 m — which is fine for a
thing the player walks up to, and waste on a thing they only ever drive past.

One asset ticket says it exactly right: *"silhouette matters more than shading."*

## Anti-patterns

Things that look like good modelling elsewhere and are wrong here:

- **Baking an AO or grunge map.** There is no AO in this renderer. Model the shadow or skip it.
- **Smoothing groups / subdivision to "clean up" faceting.** The faceting is the style.
- **A texture atlas for a single prop.** Flat colours need no UVs at all.
- **PBR metal/rough maps.** One roughness scalar per material, metalness 0.
- **Beveling every edge.** Bevels cost tris and read as nothing at 20 m. The trailer has none.
- **Modelling a whole interior.** Model only what the glass shows; section off or curtain the
  rest (rule 7). An opening with no glass and nothing behind it gets capped.
- **Matching a photo reference literally.** References set proportion and silhouette. Translate the
  detail into geometry or drop it — do not chase it into a texture.
- **Adding a material "just for this one bit."** That is a draw call. Justify it or reuse.

## Glass and interiors — worked examples

Three assets carry glass and an interior: **`broken-car.glb` (ASSET-18, 2026-08-05)**,
**`winnebago.glb` (ASSET-09, 2026-08-13)** and **`ranger.glb` (ASSET-34, 2026-08-25)**, the hero
vehicle. Each was granted as an exception to the old "no transparency" and "no interiors" rules.
Since the 2026-10-06 rewrite of rule 7 they are ordinary uses of it. They are kept here as the
record of what each one models, at what opacity, and why. The hero truck's selective rounding is
still a real exception to the bevel anti-pattern.

| Rule | What it does | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 7, no transparency | `CarGlass` is `alphaMode: BLEND` at 0.72, `doubleSided: true` | The car is a POI placed a handful of times, not scatter density, so one extra sorted draw call is affordable |
| Anti-pattern, no interiors | Low-detail tub to the beltline, two bench seats, dash, steering wheel | Once the glass is transparent, an empty shell reads worse than no glass at all |

**`winnebago.glb` (2026-08-13)** does the same: `RVGlass` is
`alphaMode: BLEND` at **0.45** (lighter than the car's 0.72 — these panes sit over closed pleated
curtains and a modelled cab, and at 0.72 the curtains black out), and the cab carries a minimal
interior (dash, wheel, two chairs breaking the beltline). Its camper windows are NOT openings —
curtain + glass sit proud of a sealed hull, so only the cab needed the interior. One extra wrinkle:
`RVCurtain` exports doubleSided because open zigzag pleat sheets have no reliable winding.

### The hero vehicle (`ranger.glb`, 2026-08-25)

The player's own truck is a fourth role, and the only asset in it. Every budget rule above is set by
*how often a thing repeats*; this one is set by **how long the player looks at it** — it is on screen
every frame of every session, and the player sits **inside** it. So the usual "silhouette survives,
surface detail does not" calculus is only half the job: the cockpit camera reads the dash at 0.5 m.

| Rule | Override | Why it was allowed |
|---|---|---|
| 7, no transparency | `RangerGlass` is `alphaMode: BLEND` at **0.45**, `doubleSided: true` | Lighter than the car's 0.72 and the RV's 0.45 is matched, because this cabin is meant to be looked *into from outside* and *out of from inside*. Six panes, one sorted material, on one object |
| Anti-pattern, no interiors | Dash with binnacle and centre stack, console, shifter, two seats, door cards with armrests, headliner, visors, mirror, and a steering wheel | **The cockpit camera's reason to exist** (owner, 2026-08-25). An interior the player sits in is not decoration |
| Anti-pattern, "beveling every edge" | SELECTIVE rounding: a two-ring domed rim at the nose, a barrel-curved face, round-section bumpers, chamfered seat and mirror sections | Owner, 2026-08-25: "the truck ends abruptly on a flat wall at both the nose and tail… the bumpers are especially rounded". The rule stands where it was aimed — the door flanks, bedsides and hood are still crisp, and were *merged* in the same pass. Rounding is spent only where the eye lands |

**Where the roundness went, and why it is not a bevel pass.** The nose gets a two-ring rim that
wraps the flanks and hood into the face, a face that is *barrel-curved in plan* (`face_y()`), and
every part mounted on it — grille bars, lamp lenses, valance — is swept along that same curve, so
the assembly cannot delaminate. Both bumpers have a seven-point round section and stand proud. The
door flanks, bedsides and hood stayed flat and were *simplified* in the same pass
(`simplify_stations()` drops any station whose ring is within a millimetre of its neighbours'
interpolation). Net: +470 tris, all of it at the two ends the eye actually lands on.

**One line, three uses.** The A-pillar's frame, the windscreen's outboard edge and the door glass's
front edge all come off a single line via `pillar_frame()`. Drawn as three independent hand-typed
lines they were a few millimetres out of parallel, and the join read as a wobble that no amount of
nudging fixed — the error was structural, not numeric.

Two things this one adds that the other two did not:

- **The steering wheel is its own node**, named `SteeringWheel`, parented to the body, with its
  origin ON the column axis — so it can be animated later without a re-export. The trap: it must be
  a *child of the body*, never a root sibling, because `src/vehicle-model.js` hides root children
  much smaller than the body (that is how it strips a model's wheels). And its rotation axis in
  three.js is node-local **Y**, not Z: the exporter's +Y-up conversion maps the Blender local Z it
  was built about onto glTF local Y.
- **Transforms are applied to the body and glass but NOT to the wheel.** Applying location to the
  wheel would move its origin to the world centre and animating it would orbit the whole truck.

**The selective rounding is scoped to the hero truck.** Anything else wanting it is a fresh ruling.

Two things worth stealing from these for any interior:

- **The beltline sightline rule.** Anything meant to be seen through a window must break the
  beltline. A seat back modelled to a realistic 0.96 m sits entirely below a 1.02 m belt and the
  cabin reads as empty no matter how good the geometry is.
- **Interior value.** Read through glass against a bright flank, only the value gap survives. The
  tub is near-black (0.030 linear), far darker than a real tan interior — the same reasoning as the
  linear-value trap in rule 5.

## Before you export

- [ ] Zero images in the file, unless it is genuinely printed information
- [ ] Every face flat-shaded
- [ ] Evaluated tri count inside the ticket budget, reported per object
- [ ] Metalness 0; roughness carries the surface difference
- [ ] Materials counted, and any split above ~6 justified in the ticket
- [ ] Dark values checked **rendered**, not as tuples
- [ ] No inverted windings — prove it, don't eyeball it. Cast rays from outside and assert the first
      face hit points back at the ray (the trailer ticket has a worked example). A model viewed from
      the far side looks mirrored when it is fine, and fine when it is mirrored.
- [ ] Silhouette still reads with the model shrunk to thumbnail size

Then follow `ASSETS.md` for format, path, pivot, forward axis, and the provenance trio
(`.glb` + `.blend` + `build.py`).
