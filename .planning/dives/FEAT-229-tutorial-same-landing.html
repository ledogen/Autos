2. The clock inside the tutorial

The day from 07:00 to bedtime at about 21:30 is about 14.5 in-game hours. The tutorial is about 20 to 25 real minutes of play. So the hours have to be spent somehow, and that choice applies to every option.

  • Held, with stamps (as the beat lists assume): the clock does not move while you drive, as today's evening leg. Each stop sets a written time. The tow's 3.2 h wait (3 h + 0.5 h per km) already exists. One more long jump is needed, about 6 to 7 h, at one stop, under its card (the "jump" rows). Every player sees the same sky at the same beat. The cost is that jump.
  • Running, as in free will: the sky moves smoothly, but at one in-game hour per real minute the night lessons land wherever the driver's pace puts them. Revision 1 worked this through: at 35 km/h the player gets home near 03:00; at 70 km/h, home in sunlight with nothing for the headlights to teach.
  • Running, with floors: the clock runs, and a stop lifts it to at least its stamp if the player is early. It guarantees the dark lessons. A slow player still overshoots.

The seam into day 0 is the same in all four: mom's sleep card already presets the wake to 07:00 day 0 (_eveWakeH in src/main.js). In options 1, 2 and 4 the short day-0 drive is held at 07:00, so free will still opens at 07:00.

Energy follows the clock. Fired at 07:00 rested, the player is sleepy at 19:00 (steering ×0.75) and tired at 21:00, so every option's night drive home steers slower. It could be a free lesson in tiredness just before mom's bed. Or energy could be held full for the whole tutorial, as today's evening holds it.

8. Recommendation

Option 1: the burger joint, errands first, with the held-and-stamped clock. It is your preference and your own inbox order, with one change: the check waits for the morning. That one change keeps every standing record as written. The tutorial still ends where the intro ends, with the last paycheck (DEC-150, 152, 153), Larry calls right after it (DEC-155), and both players start at 07:00 beside the joint just as its jobs open (DEC-156). The first five minutes are a calm drive and an errand; the trouble is the last act, and the tow carries it into the dark as today. I found no real problem with the joint.

Its weak point is the clock. To bring the flat into the evening, the camping area has to jump the clock about 6.5 h under its card, which may feel like the old 19:30 jump moved to a new place. After the sleep, the drive back to the joint teaches nothing new. If build size matters more than pacing, option 2 is about the same with a smaller rebuild, but the breakdown arrives in the first five minutes.
