FEAT-69: what a region-1 run earns, spends and takes, recounted

FEAT-69 still owes the alpha a recount of the point budget for one region. This page is that recount, as a proposal. Nothing in the game was changed. The numbers are yours to set. Each section ends with one question.

Method, in one paragraph. Every recorded drive in the repo was re-graded against today's par, using the game's own par code. Jobs were drawn from the real offer board, 4954 offers over 300 seeded boards: their type, load, traits and pay multipliers. Each job got a drive pace from the corpus and a route length from the corpus, scaled so the average par is the measured 234 s. Pay and good deeds use src/economy.js's own functions. Costs use the shop prices and wear rates in data/ and src/damage.js. Where the game has no number (how hard you drive between jobs, say) the script states an assumption in one block (ASSUME). Those assumptions are the weak spots, and section 6 lists them. A Monte Carlo run is a simulation repeated many times with random draws: here 20,000 jobs per row and 5,000 runs per row. Results are given as p10 / p50 / p90, meaning the 10th, 50th and 90th percentile: one run in ten is worse than p10.

Is the corpus stale? Not on par: every drive is re-priced exactly, so its grade is today's grade. It is stale in three other ways:

  • It is one driver. 15 of the files are tagged with your name. The rest are untagged, and nobody else has recorded a drive.
  • Every drive is a timed point-to-point drive. None is a freight, fragile, haul or demolition job. None is driven at night, in the wet, loaded, tired, or in a damaged truck.
  • The drives carry no costs. The only multi-day evidence is your 2026-11-05 run (6 deeds by day 7). That run predates hunger being on (11-07), oil changes (11-13), the oil and filter lifetimes (11-14) and the food and camp-gear prices (11-16). Its money is stale.

Two of those numbers do not depend on driving at all.

Freight is ½ a deed 90 % of the time. Freight's B line is 285 kg posted. The median freight load is 43 kg (p75 175 kg, p90 240 kg). Only 10 % of freight posts reach 285 kg, and 2 % reach A (570 kg). Freight also has the lowest net pay, because its tip is halved. So a light freight job is the worst job on the board on both counts. Freight's lines are still marked PROVISIONAL in economy.js. They also gate freight's item reward: under 285 kg there is none.

Recommendation: lower B to 100 kg and leave S and A where they are. Then a heavier load earns the deed and the item, and freight is no longer the worst choice on the board. Freight's deeds per job would go from 0.54 to about 0.7.

A day's costs were authored against an anchor of $7.50 on day 1: 1.5 × the average job's base pay (economy.js header). That anchor was set before food, oil changes and the filter's new lifetime existed. Today's running cost of a day, at day-1 prices:

Everything above is multiplied by the day tier, 1.065day − 1: × 1.46 on day 7 and × 2.0 on day 12. Tires, brakes and engine wear add almost nothing in a run. A standard tire lasts about 5 real hours of hard driving, and a run is about 1 real hour of driving in total. Crashes are not in these totals. Section 6 covers them.

So a day costs 1.4 to 2.0 × its anchor. Food alone, $8, is more than the whole anchor. Food is a fixed cost per day: it does not care how many jobs you drove. That makes it the line that punishes a slow day.

The five requisition lines pay nothing. In the model a line takes a job slot. The player takes a paid job instead whenever the wallet is short of two days of food. A run that "never finishes" is one stuck on that treadmill: every slot goes to a paid job just to eat. At pace ×1.3, 3 jobs a day finishes 98 % of runs, but 2 jobs a day finishes only 6 %.

How many jobs a day a real player drives is the one number the model can't supply. The clock allows about 2.4 jobs at your pace. Your 11-05 run made 6 deeds in 7 days, which is about 1 to 1.5 jobs a day. That run is one sample (DEC-090) and predates food, but it is the only marker there is. The economy has to work at whatever pace you intend players to play.

Recommendation: balance for 2 jobs a day, and let the recorded multi-day run confirm it. 2 is between the clock's limit (2.4) and your 11-05 run. It leaves time for the world's other content.

The cheapest food is set mostly by your own prices: the $5 chili dog, $5 groceries and $5 burger. The $2 potato and $5 dinner are [DEFAULT]. So "food $0.35 per hour" has to come from your numbers. One way: cooked groceries hold 14 h instead of 10 ($0.36 per hour). Another: chili dog and groceries at $3.50. The [DEFAULT] oil change, $8, is the other line worth moving, and so is its 50 % knee (also [DEFAULT]). Camp gear is not in the daily totals: it is a one-off. The whole shelf (blanket $4 through Rucksack $25) is about $124 on day 1. At 2 jobs a day the surplus is about $6.50 a day, so the full kit is out of reach in a 12-day run.

Recommendation: cut costs (the bold row). It keeps your $5 / $10 pay anchor and the 6.5 % push. It puts the day where costs overtake on day 12, the planned length. Which food numbers move is yours. Pay + 30 % does the same for the run, but every card on the board changes. A softer tier only moves the problem later.

6. How long a run takes, and the 12-day aim

You ruled that 12 days includes the expedition camp's lines (2026-11-06), and the camp opens at 6 deeds (DEC-120). At 0.83 deeds a job, 6 deeds is about 7 jobs at your pace (8 at ×1.3, 10–11 at ×1.5). Then come the 5 unpaid lines. So a run is about 12 to 16 job slots.

  • Every awake hour worked: the camp opens on day 3 and the run ends on day 5 (p90 day 6).
  • 2 jobs a day: the camp opens on day 4 and the run ends on day 7. That is with costs cut, so it does not stall.
  • To reach about 12 days at 2 jobs a day, the gate needs about 12 deeds: the camp opens on day 8 and the run ends on day 10 or 11. With 10 deeds the camp opens on day 6 and the run ends on day 9. These are at today's costs, so most of those runs stall. With costs cut they finish.

A player who grinds finishes region 1 in about 5 days. The 12-day run happens only if the player does about one job a day, and today's prices break that player on day 1.

Recommendation: keep 6 until the recorded run, because n = 1 cuts both ways. Fix the costs (section 5) first. If the recorded run shows 2 or more jobs a day, raise the gate toward 10–12. EXPEDITION.days (12, the grade's par day) should follow whatever the run length becomes.

7. What the model does not know, and what the recorded run should measure

These are the ASSUME block's estimates. A recorded multi-day run (FEAT-69's other owed item) can replace each one with a measurement:

  • Jobs a day, and the hours between jobs (assumed 2 h to the next giver, 1.5 h a day for camp and meals). This is the biggest unknown.
  • Engine load, which sets the oil and filter cost (assumed 0.35 on a job, 0.2 between). The damage tracks already record oil and filter %: their % per day answers it.
  • Fuel per km (assumed 0.29 gal/km). The pump spend per day answers it.
  • Crashes: plugs, bent wheels, tows. They are not in the totals. $5 a day of them moves the crossover from day 8 to day 4.
  • Letters on fragile and demolition jobs (assumed 70 % B or better). No drive records them.
  • What food a player actually buys. A player who never cooks pays $0.625 an hour, not $0.50.

To re-run after any answer: node test/budget-recount.mjs. It takes about one second and is not a gate. The levers are its LEVERS list.
