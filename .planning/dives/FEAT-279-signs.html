Road signs that tell the truth about the road ahead

You asked for a proposal to make the sign system work: little signs before hairpins, steep grades and the like that match what you are about to drive. Nothing is built yet. This page gives what signs do today, measured; the one rule the system stands on; a menu of tiers to pick from; and the decisions, each under the part it depends on. The first option of each question is the recommendation. It is your call, not mine.

Every number comes from a headless census of 12 random seeds (2069442103, 700250272, 1571169822, 1500982618, 453603903, 546567621, 1446648795, 631401382, 409343447, 1495008713, 2093458555, 252044885). Each seed's story region was built the way the game builds it: 552 km of road in total, 46 km per region. "Per km per direction" means per kilometre of driving one way, since a sign faces one direction of traffic.

1. What signs do today

The world places no signs. The seven sign models (ASSET-31) shipped on 2026-08-24, and the placement ticket (FEAT-72) was never built. So signs per km is zero for every type, on every seed.

The only signs a player meets are demolition targets:

  • The work site's "old sign line" stands 4 to 6 signs on the right shoulder of the last kilometre of the job's route, facing the traffic. Each sign's type is drawn at random from all seven. 3. The principle

A sign is a reading of the road's real geometry, made by the generator. It is never decoration scattered for look. The road system already knows the truth at build time: each run's centreline gives the bend radius and direction, the graded deck gives the grade, the network graph gives the junction ahead and its legs, and the run carries its tunnel spans. A sign module reads those and places a sign only where its claim holds. This is your standing preference that the world's character should emerge from the model, applied to signs: a twisty road gets more signs because it is twisty.

Three consequences:

  • A gate proves it. (A gate is one of the automated headless tests npm test runs.) It walks every placed sign and checks its claim against the centreline, and it fails on any false sign. FEAT-72 already asks for this.
  • Same seed, same signs. Placement is a pure function of the seed and the road (a pure function computes only from its inputs, with no randomness of its own). Re-streaming a region reproduces it exactly (SM-INV-12).
  • A fixed reference, never the truck. The brief suggested an advisory speed from the truck's lateral limit. That cannot work: the truck is a random jalopy each run (SM-INV-7), and the world may not read the run (SM-INV-12). A sign's number must come from a fixed constant, the way par does (SM-INV-2).

Tier 2: new sign faces for the hazards Tier 1 cannot mark

One new asset ticket. The sign generator (assets/models/src/road-signs.py) was built so that a new type is an outline plus a drawing function, not a new mesh. Each face is one baked 512 × 512 image, about 27 kB.

  • Single bends: Turn (W1-1, sharp, an angled arrow), Curve (W1-2, gentle), Hairpin (W1-11). Mirrored for left and right. These are the hairpin signs you asked for.
  • Advisory speed plaque (W13-1P, the yellow square under a curve sign, "25 MPH"). One plaque per number: 10 to 50 MPH in steps of 5 is 9 plaques.
  • Grade percent plaque (W7-3P, "15 %"), under the downgrade sign. About 5 plaques.
  • Side road (W2-2), for the through road at a T it cannot see.
  • Possibly: Rough road, Dip, Tunnel, Road may flood (causeways), Dead end. Each is honest from the data, but see the questions: some are too common to be worth a sign.

The advisory speed and where the sign stands

The advisory speed is the bend's speed at a fixed lateral acceleration a: vadv = √(a · R), rounded down to 5 MPH. The sign stands far enough ahead to react and brake comfortably from the speed you arrive at: d = va · tr + (va² − vadv²) / (2 · b), with reaction time tr = 1.5 s and a comfortable deceleration b = 3.4 m/s². The arrival speed va is par's reference accelerating out of the previous corner, so it too is pure road geometry.

The choice is the lateral acceleration a. Here is what each gives, in MPH, rounded down to 5:

At 0.25 g the signs read like real ones, and a player soon learns to add 10 to 15 MPH, as real drivers do. At 0.72 g the sign is the edge of grip, so taking a bend "at the sign" means sliding. 0.5 g is a speed a competent driver can always make, with a margin to beat. An advisory speed is not a countdown, so it does not break SM-INV-3, but at 0.72 g it would print par's corner speed on the road.

The kit is Californian and the speedometer's main dial is MPH, so I recommend MPH in steps of 5.

Tier 3: guide signs (where things are)

POI type and distance ("GAS 2 MI"), place names, arrows to town at junctions. Two standing rulings push against this tier:

  • Navigation is earned. The GPS is an uncommon per-run item: "until you find it you navigate via the map alone" (items.md, ratified). Arrows and distances at junctions hand out for free what the GPS sells.
  • You cut these once already. The 2026-08-23 ASSET-31 rebrief dropped the fingerpost, route shields and mile markers. FEAT-72 also says a sign never names the active job's destination.

The one guide sign that does not navigate is a name board at the place itself, a landmark you see on arrival. It needs one new sign per POI type and gives no direction.

Regulatory: speed limits

Nothing in the game enforces a speed. There is no traffic and no police yet. A limit sign would be a number with no consequence, and par already says how fast you should be. STOP signs are different: they mark which road is the minor one, which is true geometry, even though nothing makes you stop.

Gameplay hooks

  • Knockable. The sign's collider is already only its 89 mm post. The demolition system already turns a sign into a rigid body with mass when touched (13 kg, DEC-394's approach for lawn ornaments). Placed signs can reuse it.
  • Navigation without the GPS. Warning signs help you read the road, not find your way. They support the literacy that survives death (SM-INV-8): a player learns what a 15 MPH plaque means on this truck.
  • Landmarks in a giver's lines ("turn at the stop sign") would need dialogue that reads the sign placement. I have not proposed it: nothing asks for it yet.

5. The decisions

Which tiers

Recommended: Tier 1 first, then Tier 2's single-bend and hairpin signs with the advisory plaque. That is the part you asked for ("hairpin turns, steep grades"), and Tier 1 alone cannot mark a single sharp bend.

Which bends get a sign

Measured densities per km per direction: every bend needing ≥ 15 km/h of slowing, 1.48. Needing ≥ 30 km/h, 0.85. Only hairpins and winding series, about 0.4.

Which grades get a sign

A grade sign faces downhill traffic, at the top. Densities: ≥ 15 % held for ≥ 150 m, 0.36 per km. Held for ≥ 200 m, 0.16 per km. ≥ 10 % held for ≥ 50 m, 2.6 per km (too many).

Which junctions get a sign

Half the junction approaches (51 %) cannot see the junction from stopping distance.

Rock slides

FEAT-72 held the rock slide sign back until something could vouch for it. FEAT-26's slide finder now does: it accepts a road station only where the side slope rises steeply off the road. A sign there says "this slope can drop rocks", which is true whether or not a slide ever fires.

Knocking signs down

The demolition "old sign line"

Today 7 in 8 of its signs lie (section 1). Once real signs exist, a lying sign beside a true one would undo the whole system's trust. The simplest fix that never lies is to stand the old signs with their backs to the traffic, waiting to be cleared. The alternative is to draw each old sign's type from what is true where it stands, which costs more and can come up short of the job's count.

When

The ticket leaves this open. Signs depend on FEAT-278's clutter stream (built after the loading screen), so they land after it or with it.

6. Anything this misses

Hazards I measured but did not propose a sign for, because they are common or a defect: tunnels (0.32 per km), causeways (0.66 per km), rough road (a third of all road), dead ends (11 in 552 km) and sags. A sag that bottoms a new truck is a road defect to fix (BUG-216), not something to sign. Say here if you want any of them signed.
