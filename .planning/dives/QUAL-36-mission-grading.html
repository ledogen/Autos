Mission grading: one clock, one grammar, six identities

You asked how every mission type is paid, graded and rewarded, and for a few levers that make it read as one system. Everything below is read from the code on feature/content (merged with main at e26da3d2), not from the design notes. Where the two disagree, the list in section 4 says so. Every number has a source in section 7.

The worked examples come from real offers, rolled and routed headlessly on three random seeds (3230259412, 1046436846, 2930777188; region 1, pay tier 1). They are what those boards posted, not typical values.

1. The one formula underneath every type

Every type except the papers' accuracy money runs through the same pay line (payoutFor in src/economy.js). ratio = time on the job clock ÷ par. Par is the C/D line by definition.

Times below are on the job clock: one real second is one game minute, so a 5:16 drive is also 5 h 16 min of game time.

So the tip is a clock on every type, with the same lines (0.652 / 1.0 / 1.348). Only courier and papers (and, by accident, demolition) show that clock. The letter is a separate thing: it is the type's own axis, and it decides only the deed and the optional item, never the money.

Common to every type with an item: 40 % of offers carry one. A bonus offer must plan a leg of at least 1.5 km. The item's gate follows its rarity (common B–A, uncommon A–S, rare S). Distance, pay and load never pick the item, except freight's kg. Taking it costs its shop price off the cash, capped at what the job paid.

Slow always costs pay on every type: the tip runs from whole at ratio 0.652 to none at 1.348. Only courier, fetch and papers also cost the deed.

3. What each type asks the player to be good at

  • Courier: be quick and know the roads. Time is the whole job. The deed, the item and the tip all ride the same clock, and it is the only type where the shot clock means exactly what it shows.
  • Fetch / ride: courier with an errand in it. Drive out, park at the pickup, come back. The motorist's tire rides loose in the bed, and the job will not finish until it is back in.
  • Papers: drive a tour fast and throw well. The clock grades, the throws pay. Throw money is yours even if the round fails. A B or better on the top size earns Larry's next route.
  • Fragile: drive smooth. Condition is the deed and half the base. It is the calm type: no clock on screen, and the deadline is 3 × par. Speed still pays the tip, but nothing shows it.
  • Haul: load smart and keep it in the bed. You choose the count and pay for it, so spare bundles are insurance you buy. It is the biggest earner per trip.
  • Freight: carry heavy with the truck you have. The poster sets the load, so the deed and the item are known before you leave. The job is surviving the drive, with a 4 × par deadline and half the usual tip.
  • Demolition: clear the whole shoulder. The share smashed is the deed and scales all the pay. The 40 s window between hits is its real clock.

4. What a new player trips on

  1. Demolition shows a time bar that does not grade it. The bar falls S → A → B on the clock, but the letter is the share smashed. A 100 % site in "D time" earns a full deed. (FEAT-65 calls the bar interim.)
  2. Larry's item is advertised and never paid. His offers roll an item 40 % of the time (a gate pins this, per your 10-31 "give him every item"), and the map card prints it. The paper settle never passes it on, so it can never be earned. The old FEAT-87 note "rounds carry no bonus item" is stale.
  3. Impatient / Patient lie on calm types. Their card line says "rank times 10 % tighter" on fragile, haul and freight, where it changes nothing. On courier it moves the C line to 0.9 × par, but the tip line does not move, so an Impatient job still pays half its tip at par while grading it D.
  4. "Pay based on speed" with no clock to see. Every card says it. Only courier and papers show the clock. On fragile, haul and freight the tip drains on an invisible timer, including while you stop for gas.
  5. A haul can promise a deed rung its stock cannot reach. Stock rolls 14–30, the 1-deed line is 18. A 15-bundle yard (seed 3599450461) posts "Deed: 1 if 18+ bundles delivered".
  6. The letter shows on one result card only. The paper round's result card prints it. Every POI result card prints none (your Q-3, 10-24). The casserole's prints it.
  7. Fragile par grows with mass, like freight. The code stretches any job with a strapped load. DEC-002 names freight only. Small for most items (+2 % for eggs), +16 % for the piano.
  8. The courier countdown is a par clock. Before the bar appears it prints "finish in m:ss or better for C rank", and the C line is par. DEC-003 allows it (Q-6), but it is the one place par reaches the screen as digits.

5. Four ways to unify it

Each is one or two levers. What changes for each type, what stays, and the build cost.

A. Drop the letter; let the numbers speak

Changes: the bar's bands are labelled with what they hold ("full tip", "deed 1", "deed ½", the item) instead of S/A/B/C/D. The paper and casserole result cards lose the letter. The stinger plays by the deed earned. Stays: letters inside the code (item gates, deeds, the expedition grade). Fragile, haul, freight and demolition already show no letter, so they are untouched. Cost: small: the strip's labels, two result cards, the stinger map. Downside: the five-colour S-rank chase goes, and the win card's expedition grade is still a letter. Courier's deed must then be stated as times on the card ("deed 1 if in by 4:11"), which prints a rank line as digits from accept, a bigger par leak than Q-6.

B. Hurried and calm jobs (recommended)

One lever: a job is hurried or calm, and only hurried jobs get the shot clock (the bar, the heartbeat, the music, the "finish in" countdown).

The Impatient giver says it in their line (GIVER_LINES is still empty, so this would be the first owner-written line), and the trait line reads "on the clock" instead of "rank times 10 % tighter". About 6–7 % of offers are Impatient (one trait on half the offers, Impatient one of 7–9 eligible). With ~17 live offers a board, that is about one Impatient job per board, and an Impatient calm job every two or three boards. Stays: every pay formula, every deed rule, DEC-018's "the tip is speed". Cost: small: the bar's on/off rule (one function), a tip-only label mode for the bar on calm types, the trait text, a rank-timer gate case, and your words for the line. Downside: a calm job's tip still drains while you stop for gas; B only makes that visible on the hurried ones.

C. The clock only on Impatient jobs (your literal idea)

Changes: no bar anywhere except Impatient jobs, of any type. A plain courier job's deed becomes "1 on delivery", because time no longer grades it. Stays: the tip on time. Cost: small. Downside: courier loses its only skill: about 93 % of courier jobs become "drive there". Deeds inflate, since courier is the most common offer on the sampled boards (about half) and today a slow one earns 0; the 10-deed camp gate and the ~12-day run would need re-measuring. The shot clock becomes rare (~1 per board), the opposite of "only present in one or two types".

D. Calm jobs are truly calm

Changes: fragile's tip follows condition (whole at 100 %, none at 50 % or below, the 10-15 rule) instead of time. Stopping for gas costs only the deadline. Stays: everything else. Cost: small: one payout function, the card's "based on condition", the economy gate. Downside: it reverses DEC-018 (your 10-21 ruling reversed exactly this). Health then drives the base and the tip, and a fast fragile drive earns nothing over a slow one. Works alone or on top of B.

Recommendation: B. It keeps the shot clock where time is the job, puts it on calm types only when a character asks for it, and fixes the demolition mismatch with no pay change. Take A's labels on the bar if you want to drop the letters too (question 3). The bugs in section 4 (Larry's item, the trait text) get fixed either way.
